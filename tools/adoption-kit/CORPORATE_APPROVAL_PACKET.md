# Corporate Approval Packet — Open-Pact License (OPL-1.4)

*Decision document for legal / procurement / architecture-review boards
evaluating OPL adoption or use of an OPL-licensed dependency. Every material
claim cites a license section. Canonical text: `LICENSE.md` (OPL-1.4.1). This
Packet is generated by `make_kit.py` from the worked example and the license
text; regenerate after any license change. Fill the sign-off block (§9) per
adoption — the rest is the standard template.*

---

## 1. Summary
OPL is a **fair-source, two-tier** license: **free for personal/non-revenue use**,
**paid for commercial use**, with the **Maintainer setting the price and payment
mechanism**. It is **not** OSI open source and **not** under any external
registry. It is independently governed by the Open-Pact-Standard org (Ikaros
Digital LLC). Conversion to Apache-2.0 is automatic on abandonment (§5) and, if
opted in, on a scheduled DOSP date (§5.1).

## 2. License model
- Two tiers: **Personal Use** (free, no gate) and **Commercial Use** (paid per
  Standard Terms). Polyform Mixed model (§3.3).
- **No copyleft.** The Functional Equivalent restriction was removed in v1.4 (§3.6
  note). No reciprocity / "infect" clause.

## 3. Obligations on commercial users (§4, §13)
- Maintain a **reachable contact** (§4.1).
- Maintain a **Valid Standard Terms URL** or declare Personal-Use-only (§4.3).
- **Pay** per the Maintainer's published Standard Terms (§3.3).
- Per-Version commercial terms may be pinned immutably in `COMMERCIAL_TERMS.md`
  (§13) — that file is the binding contract for that Version.

## 4. Commercial Use mechanics (§3.3, §3.7)
- Commercial Use = use in the course of business / revenue generation.
- Payment flows to the Maintainer via their chosen mechanism (Stripe, bank
  transfer, x402 crypto, etc.) — **no intermediary, no fee to OPL**.
- If the Maintainer revokes or materially changes Standard Terms, existing
  commercial users get a **90-day wind-down** (§3.7).

## 5. Conversion triggers (§5, §5.1) — the escape hatches
- **Abandonment (§5):** if no Maintainer is reachable for the declared period
  (default **36 months**), the Maintainer **grants each recipient an Apache-2.0
  license, effective automatically** — a *standing conditional grant* that
  requires **no actor, successor, or registry**. The fallback works even if the
  Maintainer and any Successor have vanished.
  - *Optional Designated Successor (Maintainer's choice, never required):* if
    `NOTICE` names one, they are **authorized but not required** to *record* the
    conversion by publishing an Apache-2.0 `LICENSE`. The recording is
    conclusive evidence but **not a precondition**. A Successor may only record
    the predefined Apache-2.0 terms — they cannot change price, modify the Work,
    or revoke licenses. This makes OPL the most abandonment-robust fair-source
    license: automatic by its own grant, human-recordable if the Maintainer opts
    in, zero new infrastructure.
- **DOSP (§5.1, opt-in):** if `NOTICE` declares a DOSP period, each Version
  converts to Apache-2.0 N months after first public release. **Silent NOTICE =
  no scheduled conversion** (opt-out by default). The optional Successor may
  record this trigger too.
- Both land on **Apache License 2.0** — universally understood by scanners and
  registries. This is the interop bridge (see `INTEROPERABILITY.md`).

## 6. Risk assessment
| Risk | Assessment | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Copyleft / reciprocity | **None** | §3.6 (removed) |
| Patent | Royalty-free grant from Contributors; terminates vs patent asserters | §6 |
| Termination of *granted* rights | **None** for the Version licensed; the Maintainer may later decline to license *new* commercial use or change Standard Terms (existing users keep rights via 90-day wind-down, §3.7) | §3.7, §5, §5.1 |
| Maintainer vanishes → hostage | **No** — deemed conversion to Apache-2.0 (see §5 note) | §5 |
| Indemnification | **None provided.** Work is "AS IS"; no indemnity from Maintainer (same as most OSS) | §7, §8 |
| GPL compatibility | **Incompatible** — do not combine into GPL | FAQ §8 |
| Forced open-source publication | **No** — conversion is opt-in (DOSP) or abandonment-only | §5.1 |
| Registry / on-chain dependency | **None** | §13, README |

## 7. Red lines (non-negotiable, by license design)
- **No forced rolling open-source conversion.** OPL is source-available by
  default; scheduled conversion is the Maintainer's explicit choice (§5.1).
- **Creator autonomy paramount.** The Maintainer sets price, payment, DOSP, AI.
- **Zero infrastructure.** No registry, token, DAO, or on-chain requirement.

## 8. Worked example (evidence the tooling is real)
See `worked-example/` — the **origin-canary** project (a Rust forensic
canary-embedder), adopted under OPL-1.4 with a real `NOTICE`,
`COMMERCIAL_TERMS.md`, and `LICENSE`. Operational notes for reviewers:

- **What it does:** embeds cryptographically-signed provenance markers
  ("canaries") into source trees for theft/tamper detection.
- **No network calls, no telemetry, no on-chain dependency** — it runs fully
  offline. Suitable as a build-time dependency.
- **Tests:** a **90-test suite** including **tamper-detection**
  (`verify_integrity`) and **theft-detection** (`verify_source`), proving the
  adoption tooling is sound, not a toy.
- **Standard Terms URL** in the example (`https://ikaros.dev/terms`) is the
  Maintainer's own, illustrative, and **Maintainer-controlled** — when you
  adopt OPL, you publish *your* own Standard Terms URL; the example is not a
  live pricing commitment.
