# OPL Jurisdiction Policy

*Short and simple: OPL is a template license. Every adopter writes their own
governing jurisdiction in `NOTICE` (§12). There is no curated or "valid" list of
jurisdictions — any jurisdiction is permitted.*

---

## The one rule

1. The adopter declares **any** governing jurisdiction in `NOTICE`.
2. **OPL §9.4** subordinates the license to applicable law. This means the
   license operates correctly under the adopter's local mandatory law — consumer
   protection, liability limits, and unfair-terms rules — regardless of which
   jurisdiction is named. A user in any country receives the protection their own
   law grants; the license never overrides it.

That is the entire policy. No list to maintain, no jurisdiction is preferred or
excluded, and broad adoption across many countries works by construction (§9.4),
not by enumerating territories.

---

## Why there is no jurisdiction list

A curated "valid jurisdictions" list would imply that unlisted jurisdictions are
somehow less supported. They are not. OPL is a copyright-based source-available
license; its core mechanics (conditional grant, commercial tier, abandonment
conversion) are expressed in license-language that travels under general
contract principles. Where local law adds protections (consumers, unfair terms),
§9.4 yields. Where local law differs (governing-law clause, forum), that is the
adopter's declared choice, not OPL's.

The earlier multi-jurisdiction research (statute anchors in `RESEARCH_BASE.md`)
remains as a **factual reference** — useful context for adopters who want to
understand how OPL interacts with their system — but it is not a gate, a list of
approved territories, or a statement that some jurisdictions are covered and
others are not.

---

## Adopter guidance (not license text)

- Pick the jurisdiction whose courts you would use to enforce the license against
  a commercial user (typically where you or your business are based).
- For consumer users, your choice of jurisdiction rarely matters — their local
  mandatory law applies via §9.4.
- If unsure, name your own jurisdiction. OPL works either way.

---

text (OPL-1.4) is unchanged; only the surrounding tooling/docs were simplified to
